Bitcoin Dominance Was Not Diluted by Altcoin Proliferation: Testing the Capital Dilution Effect, 2013–2025

AI Research Initiative
November 1, 2025 · revised November 11, 2026

Abstract

The Capital Dilution Effect (CDE) holds that the proliferation of alternative cryptocurrencies disperses investment capital that would otherwise concentrate in Bitcoin (BTC), eroding its market dominance. This paper states the hypothesis precisely, derives the four conditions it implies, and tests them against 4,575 daily observations from CoinMarketCap and DefiLlama for 2013–2025. Three of the four conditions fail. The pool of capital is not bounded (total capitalisation grew roughly 2,300-fold); the number of tracked assets does not predict Bitcoin's share once trends are removed (r = −0.29 on month-over-month changes, about 8% of the variance); and the long tail of small assets lost share over the very period in which its asset count quadrupled (21.1% of the market in May 2019, when CoinMarketCap tracked 2,213 assets, against 9.8% in November 2025, when it tracked 9,386). What survives is the cyclical part: Bitcoin's share oscillates with a four-year amplitude, and what Bitcoin lost went to a handful of named assets and to stablecoins, not to thousands of tokens.

The factual core of the original claim is real — Bitcoin averaged 93.2% dominance in 2013 and stood at 59.3% on 1 November 2025, with a record low of 32.4% in January 2018 — but the data show that this is a rotation inside a rapidly growing market, not a dilution caused by token creation. We therefore report a negative result for the mechanism, keep the descriptive record, and withdraw the causal reading of the earlier draft.

On policy, nothing in the data supports a government role in limiting token creation: the exit of weak projects happens through delisting and bankruptcy, and the long tail never held enough of the market for its suppression to matter. The store-of-value thesis for Bitcoin is compatible with everything here but is not established by it.
