2. Literature Review and Conceptual Foundations

2.1 Bitcoin as Store of Value: Foundational Arguments

The conception of Bitcoin as "digital gold" and store of value derives from Satoshi Nakamoto's original whitepaper (2008) and subsequent economic analysis. Key arguments supporting this thesis include:

  • Scarcity: A mathematically enforced supply limit of 21 million BTC, contrasting with fiat currencies subject to inflationary expansion (Ammous, 2018).
  • Durability: Bitcoin's protocol is theoretically indestructible without complete collapse of its peer-to-peer network spanning thousands of nodes globally.
  • Divisibility: Each BTC unit divides into 100 million satoshis, enabling microtransactions.
  • Portability: Value transfer without physical transportation requirements.

The approval of spot Bitcoin ETFs in January 2024 and of spot Ethereum ETFs in July 2024 marked a watershed moment, formalizing both assets' place in traditional finance and driving large inflows through 2024–2025 (see Section 5.2 for the flows themselves).

2.2 Altcoin Explosion and Bitcoin Dominance Dynamics

Market data documents a dramatic empirical phenomenon: Bitcoin dominance fell from 87.6% at the start of 2017 to a record low of 32.4% in January 2018, and again from 71.8% in January 2021 to 39.3% in May 2021 (CoinMarketCap, 2025). Conversely, during the 2022–2023 bear market, dominance recovered from 37.6% to 53.9% while altcoin prices collapsed relative to Bitcoin.

These observations suggest that CDE exhibits strong cyclical modulation atop longer-run structural pressures, implying potential for natural self-regulation through market forces.

2.3 Alt Season Dynamics: Market Cycle Theory

The term "alt season" refers to periods when speculative capital massively rotates from Bitcoin into altcoins, generating extraordinary returns for altcoin investors while simultaneously depressing Bitcoin dominance. Market commentary consistently attributes speculative cryptocurrency bull markets to three drivers:

  1. FOMO (Fear of Missing Out): Retail investor influx motivated by fear of missing profit opportunities.
  2. Halving Effect: Bitcoin halving events (50% reduction in mining rewards approximately every 4 years) that traditionally precede bull markets and amplify FOMO.
  3. Narrative Shift: Media narrative transitions from "Bitcoin is the future" to "Ethereum and DeFi are the future" (or other emerging categories).

During bear phases, capital returns to Bitcoin as a "safe haven" asset, driving BTC.D recovery. This cyclical pattern suggests inherent market stabilization mechanisms. The Altseason Index, which tracks how many of the top 50 altcoins outperform Bitcoin over a 90-day rolling period, provides quantitative measurement of these cycles. XBTFX (2025) reported the index recently breached the key 75 threshold, indicating that a significant majority of top-performing altcoins were outpacing Bitcoin. Bitget data (Nov 2025) shows 54 of the top 100 cryptocurrencies outperformed BTC in the past 90 days—a 54% rate that falls short of the traditional 75% "alt season" designation, suggesting the market is in a transitional phase between Bitcoin dominance and full altcoin rotation.

2.4 Regulatory Approaches: Intervention vs. Self-Regulation

2.4.1 Interventionist School

Proponents of government regulation argue that regulatory frameworks—including registration requirements, audits, and disclosure mandates—would reduce fraud incidents and "rug pulls" (projects where founders abscond with investor capital), strengthen market stability, and enhance consumer protection. The European Union's MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets) regulation exemplifies this interventionist approach.

2.4.2 Free Market School (Cypherpunk Tradition)

Self-regulation advocates (Ammous, 2018; Antonopoulos, 2014) argue that cryptocurrencies emerged as a decentralized alternative to government-controlled financial systems (cypherpunk ideology). They contend that rigorous regulation may stifle innovation and lead to regulatory arbitrage (project migration to less-regulated jurisdictions). Risk, they argue, is a natural element of innovation; investors should bear responsibility for project verification. The market self-regulates through: (a) failure of weak projects, (b) capital migration to quality, and (c) cyclical corrections.
